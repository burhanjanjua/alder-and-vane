# Alder & Vane: project explanation

I started with the design in Figma. I used a little Figma Make to explore layouts and ChatGPT to help with early ideas and wording. Once I had a direction I was happy with, I refined the design, chose free images from pexels.com and built the webpage in HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript. I then pushed the project to GitHub and deployed it on Vercel.

[Figma design](https://www.figma.com/design/ShCVzZ66q8sfdAby8sFoDw/Alder---Vane-%E2%80%94-Website-Design?node-id=3-3&t=8SVlF50clAEOF2LO-1) | [GitHub repository](https://github.com/burhanjanjua/alder-and-vane) | [Live website](https://alder-and-vane.vercel.app/)

The main goal was to get a homeowner to book a design consultation, so I put that option in the header, at the start of the page and again beside the contact details. I put services and selected projects in between to show what the team does before asking someone to get in touch. Architects, builders and designers bring in much of Alder & Vane's work, so they have a separate section with a link to ask about the design partner programme. It mentions the practical things they need from an integrator: drawings, specifications, site coordination and one person to contact. I kept the site to one page and left out a contact form; the email and phone links work directly.

I wanted the page to show what good integration feels like in a home, rather than lead with equipment. The large house photograph sets that tone straight away. Serif headings, simpler body text and a cream and charcoal palette give the site a quieter feel, with bronze used in small details. I used an A/V mark and wordmark because the brief did not provide an identity. The project images change size and position as you scroll, and the darker partner section gives the page a change of pace. Navigation is simple, the mobile layout follows the same reading order, and the small hover effects can be reduced for people who prefer less motion.

The copy uses the company facts and quotations in the brief and keeps the services understandable for someone who does not buy these systems often. I chose four images from the supplied pack: the house for the opening, the pool residence for Camelback, the stone entrance for Desert Mountain and the bar for the Old Town club. They help tell each story, but they are illustrative images from the brief, not photographs of those fictional projects. I cropped them to suit the different sections.

The site is built as a straightforward static page. It has semantic headings, image descriptions, a skip link and visible keyboard focus. The project images load lazily and have fixed dimensions to help the page load and settle cleanly. If the site grew later, the service and project sections could be turned into reusable templates. I have not completed a formal accessibility audit. For search, the title, description and page copy name the services and Scottsdale, while the LocalBusiness data uses only the contact and service areas supplied in the brief. That also gives search and answer tools clear facts to use.

**Tools used:** I worked on the design in Figma and used Figma Make briefly to try layout ideas. ChatGPT helped with early ideas and Codex helped edit. I used HTML, CSS and JavaScript for the webpage, Git and GitHub for the code, and Vercel for deployment. I selected and cropped the supplied images and reviewed the final design and copy myself.
